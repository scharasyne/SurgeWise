# SurgeWise demo script

**Three-minute walkthrough for city health planners and judges.** Start on **Dashboard**. The figures below use the four-week archive forecast at **1×**, with the default hospital settings. Leave the map on **Accumulated projected cases**.

| Time | On screen | What to say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:20 | Open **Dashboard**. Point to Quezon City and the hospital pins. | “If dengue cases rise in Quezon City, how many beds might our hospitals need? Let’s follow one forecast from the city map to a single hospital and see what the numbers mean for a planner.” |
| 0:20–0:40 | Click **Simulation** in the left navigation. Click **Historical replay** at the top left of the map. Open **Year** to show **2012–2022**, then press **Esc**. | “Our weekly dengue case history covers 2012 through 2022. We can pick any of those eleven years to replay the estimated cases by barangay. Today, I’ll start at the end of that archive and forecast the next four weeks.” |
| 0:40–1:10 | In the same panel, select **Future forecast**. Under **Projected dengue cases**, choose **Next 1–6 weeks after the archive**. Set **Forecast horizon** to **4 weeks** and **Case multiplier (×)** to **1**. Click **Run simulation**. | “We’re forecasting dengue case counts. The random forest estimates weekly cases for each of Quezon City’s 142 barangays. Adding those estimates gives us the city total. I’m keeping the multiplier at one to show the baseline. For the first week of 2023, the forecast is about **64 cases**.” |
| 1:10–1:35 | Close **Results** with the **×** in its header. Below the map, find **Play** and the slider beside it. Move the slider to **Day 7 / 28**, then to its midpoint, **Day 14 / 28**. If the slider is hidden, click **Show timeline**. | “Here’s the end of week one. Now watch the map as I move to week two. Darker shading means more projected cases have accumulated in that barangay. The first figure below the slider reads **127.9**. That’s about **128 expected cases across the city over two weeks**.” |
| 1:35–2:15 | Stay on **Day 14**. In the strip below the slider, point in order to **QC expected bed demand**, **QC staffed beds required**, and **QC additional beds needed**. | “How many of those people would need a bed on day 14? This scenario assumes 15% of cases need admission and each patient stays four days. That gives us **5.5 expected dengue inpatients** across the city that day. The next figure is **46 staffed beds**. Each hospital rounds its own share of demand up to a whole bed, and those requirements add up. The last figure is **zero additional beds**. Every hospital’s assumed capacity covers its share in this run.” |
| 2:15–2:45 | Click **Bed details ›** at the right end of the totals strip. Scroll **inside the panel** to **Hospital and barangay details**. Type **Luke** in **Search all QC hospitals** and select **ST. LUKE'S MEDICAL CENTER**. Scroll past **Registered inventory** to **Projected bed demand · day 14**. | “Let’s look at St. Luke’s. The Markov chain uses distance and hospital preference to estimate where admissions from each barangay would go. On day 14, St. Luke’s accounts for **0.12 expected beds**, a small share of the citywide demand. The line below shows that its expected demand is **up 0.2%** from a week earlier.” |
| 2:45–3:00 | Leave **Hospital details** visible and point to **Assumed dengue slots**. | “A planner can use this view to decide which hospitals to contact first. If a scenario shows a shortfall, the next step is to confirm staffed capacity with that hospital and discuss how many extra beds it could prepare.” |
